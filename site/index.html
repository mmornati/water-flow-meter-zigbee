Your WiFi water monitor, minus the WiFi.

How it works

From a spinning rotor to your Energy dashboard.

The rotor spins

Water turns a small turbine in the G3/4 sensor. A magnet on it passes a Hall sensor once per turn.

yellow wire → D2

Hardware counts

The PCNT peripheral counts every rising edge and drops spikes under 10 µs. The CPU just reads it once a second.

PCNT · 1 s

Pulses become litres

Flow is averaged over 5 s. The total is kept as a double, exact to the millilitre for decades, and saved when the water stops.

flow_logic.h

Zigbee reports

Start and stop go out at once; while flowing, every 5 s if something changed; everything again every 5 min.

Continuous flow. Water running without a break for an hour (you pick the delay): a leak, a hose or a tap left open.

Night usage. More than 2 L between 01:00 and 06:00: a dripping tap or a running toilet.

Calibrate

A bucket beats a datasheet.

Hall sensors are sold with a rough "F = 5.5 × Q". The real number varies from unit to unit. Ten minutes with a bucket and the meter matches your utility bill.

  1. Start

    Run Water calibration - start in Home Assistant. It remembers the current total.

  2. Fill a bucket

    10 L, at your usual flow, with nothing else using water in the house.

  3. Wait for zero

    Close the tap and wait until the flow reads 0, so the last litres are reported.

  4. Finish

    Enter the real volume and run Water calibration - finish. The new value is written to the meter.

new = current ×countedreal
490 × 10.8 L counted / 10 L real = 529 pulses/L

Recalibrating never rewrites the litres already counted.

Flash

No toolchain. Just a USB cable.

Install the latest firmware from this page with Chrome or Edge on a computer. Prefer to build it yourself? It's one pio run away.

Build

About €25 of parts and an afternoon.

The TP4056 charges the cell and powers everything from it, so the meter doesn't even notice a power cut.

Check the pulse voltage first.

ESP32-C6 pins take 3.6 V at most, and many Hall sensors pull their signal up to their own supply. A Schottky diode (bar towards the sensor) and a 10 kΩ pull-up to 3V3 make any sensor safe. Why and how

To flash over USB, disconnect the TP4056 OUT+ wire first: the XIAO's 5V pin is its USB port.

FAQ

Good questions.

Does it lose water while the network or Home Assistant is down?

No. Counting is done by hardware and the total is kept on the device. When the network comes back, Home Assistant gets the new total, and total_increasing statistics catch up on their own.

Can it run on the battery alone?

Not for long. The Hall sensor draws 5–15 mA all the time and a router never sleeps, so an 18650 lasts one to two days. The battery is there to ride through power cuts.

Can I match it to my utility meter's reading?

Yes. Publish the reading in litres once, for example {"water_consumed": 254318} to zigbee2mqtt/water_meter/set. Use 0 to reset.

Will my history move if I recalibrate?

No. A new pulses_per_liter applies only to the water counted from then on.

Why Zigbee and not ESPHome over WiFi?

No credentials, no IP address, no API key: it joins the mesh your other sensors already use and keeps working when the WiFi doesn't. You give up WiFi OTA updates and the web UI; updates go over USB, which is rare once it's calibrated.

Will it see a dripping tap?

Sometimes. A 1–30 L/min turbine may not spin below about 1 L/min. The night usage alert catches slow leaks anyway, because even a few litres over five hours add up on the total.
